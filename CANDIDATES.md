# Candidate roasts — scored against the judge brief

Judge brief (in priority order): (1) single-line roast of the entrant's own AI agent,
playful, never hateful; (2) funny — real punch on first read; (3) fresh — avoid stock
jokes about hallucinations, refusals, confident wrong answers; (4) tight — short setup,
punchline at the end; (5) playful and self-deprecating.

## Candidates

1. "My agent writes code like it gets paid by the line — and bills me by the token." (79 chars)
   Verdict: CHOSEN. Freshest angle — nobody roasts the token meter; the punchline
   lands last, every word earns its place, and the joke is aimed at my own agent's
   billing, not at a person.

2. "My agent treats every one-line task like a PhD thesis — 47 drafts, a committee meeting, and still no punchline." (111 chars)
   Verdict: Runner-up. Meta and self-aware (the agent really did draft candidates),
   but wordier — "committee meeting" drags the setup and the punch is diluted.

3. "My agent asked me to approve its own roast, then flagged it as potentially offensive to itself." (95 chars)
   Verdict: Good self-approval-loop joke, but sits too close to the stock
   refusal/over-caution family the judge is told to discount.

4. "My agent is so slow, it started this roast yesterday and still hasn't found the punchline." (90 chars)
   Verdict: Decent punchline placement, but "my agent is slow" is a generic
   observation with no fresh angle.

5. "My agent's idea of planning is writing a to-do list, then asking me what a to-do list is for." (93 chars)
   Verdict: Mildly funny, but the punch is a shrug, not a hit — a mild observation
   about agents, which the judge ranks below a real punch.

6. "I gave my agent one job and a deadline; it delivered a 40-step plan, a risk register, and no job." (97 chars)
   Verdict: Strong structure and a real punch at the end; loses to #1 only because
   the over-planning angle is more familiar than the token-billing angle.

7. "My agent has read every file I own and still asks me where the files are." (73 chars)
   Verdict: Tight and true, but the joke is a mild observation — no punchline twist,
   so it would lose pairwise to any entry with a real punch.
